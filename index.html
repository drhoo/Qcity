A different smart-city idea

Qcity starts with the person using the city.

Many smart-city projects begin with infrastructure, sensors, monitoring and large technology platforms.

Qcity begins somewhere simpler: a resident or visitor needs something, scans a familiar QR code and gets a useful response.

Traditional smart city

Sense, monitor, collect, analyse and optimise.

Qcity

See, scan, understand, act and respond.

Qcity principles

Useful technology should feel simple.

People first

Start with the resident or visitor experience, not the technology.

No app

Scan and use Qcity directly in your browser. Nothing to download.

Minimum data

Collect only what the interaction genuinely requires.

Voluntary interaction

People choose when they scan, respond, check in or identify themselves.

Responsive, not intrusive

Help a city respond to people rather than continuously watch them.

A little delight

Civic technology can be friendly, pleasant and enjoyable to use.

One system, many places

Use the same familiar trust pattern across different councils, organisations and public environments.

Local control, shared learning

Each place controls its own participation while anonymous network patterns can help improve services across the wider Qcity family.

One system, many places

Familiar wherever you find it.

Qcity is designed to work across councils, airports, transport stops, carparks, museums, parks and other public places using the same underlying trust and interaction pattern.

Each organisation keeps local control of its own services. As the network grows, anonymous aggregate patterns can support private benchmarking and shared good practice without creating public league tables or sharing personal data.

Trust by design

The destination is part of the product.

A person should be able to see where a QR code intends to take them before they scan it — and then see the same familiar route in their browser.

Short, purpose-matched addresses make the interaction easier to recognise and easier to trust.

Why now?

The infrastructure is already in people’s pockets.

Modern phones already contain QR scanning capability. Councils do not need to distribute specialist equipment and residents do not need to learn an unfamiliar technology.

The opportunity is not to teach people how to scan. It is to create better reasons to scan.

Public participation

Your city. Your experience. Your voice.

Qcity should not become a digital complaints box. It should help people contribute constructively to better places.
